# SEO competitive research

Audit date: 23 September 2026. Public observations are a snapshot, not a ranking guarantee.

## Method and limits

Compared public homepages, selected navigation and articles, raw HTML metadata, robots files and sitemaps. Public search queries included `"Matcha Flavoured" wiki` and domain-specific queries. The available search provider is not a controlled Google ranking test; absence from these results does not establish absence from Google's index. Search Console, traffic, backlinks and field performance were not available. Direct browser-source retrieval failed for several domains; successful public HTTP fetches supplied the observations below. A 403 response is a measurement limit, not proof that Google is blocked.

Competitor content is evidence of their presentation and coverage only. No competitor gameplay statements were adopted as facts. Gameplay changes must still be checked against the pinned pack, official release notes and developer videos.

## Priorities

1. **Resolve crawl and metadata defects before expanding content.** Ensure only canonical, indexable, successful article URLs enter the sitemap; search/404 pages must not impersonate the homepage. Check redirects, HTTP status codes and canonical consistency on the actual deployed host after release. [Google's canonical guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/consolidate-duplicate-urls) describes redirects, canonicals and sitemap inclusion as signals, not commands.
2. **Protect the existing static-content advantage.** Keep essential article content and ordinary links in initial HTML. Search controls can use JavaScript without making the whole encyclopedia depend on it. Google's rendering support does not make hash-only content routes a good substitute for separate page URLs. [JavaScript SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics).
3. **Win specific player tasks.** Existing Installation, Guide for new players, Progression, Mud Kiln, Death, Enchanting and material articles are a strong starting set. Review their first paragraphs against concrete needs: installation failure, first-day progression, furnace route, healing/death, silver, steel, seeds/saplings and recipe unlocks. Add clear question headings where they help; link the answer from relevant hubs. A separate troubleshooting page is justified only if existing installation/known-bug coverage does not already answer the need.
4. **Keep facts auditable and freshness meaningful.** Show the supported pack version, connect change-sensitive claims to primary sources and review pages after releases. Fix misleading counts and broken links. Selectively improve useful generated references before making them indexable; removing all generated-page `noindex` rules would expose unreviewed thin pages. Google prioritizes original, useful, reliable content and warns against changing dates merely to appear fresh. [Helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).
5. **Use natural titles and real descriptions.** Both spellings already appear in homepage metadata. Keep precise item titles and readable descriptions; do not repeat both spellings on every article or pad answers to a word count. [Title guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link).
6. **Measure mobile usability before claiming a performance win.** Check representative recipe tables, large catalogues and mobile navigation. Initial HTML size is not LCP, INP or CLS. Collect lab measurements now and field data when enough traffic exists; good Core Web Vitals alone do not guarantee rankings. [Core Web Vitals guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals).
7. **Earn discovery through usefulness.** Make source citations, contribution instructions and shareable deep links easy to find. Relevant community references are a plausible discovery opportunity, but this audit did not measure link authority and no outreach was performed.

## Search Console follow-up after an approved deployment

The sitemap was already submitted, according to the owner. That is appropriate for a new site. Confirm its status and last-read timestamp rather than repeatedly resubmitting an unchanged file. Sitemap submission helps URL discovery; it does not guarantee crawling, indexing or rankings. [Google sitemap documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap).

Inspect a small representative set: homepage, new-player guide, installation, Mud Kiln, Death and one detailed equipment/food article. Compare declared and Google-selected canonical, crawl access, rendered content and indexability. Request indexing only for these important corrected/new pages, then monitor the Page Indexing report. Crawling may take days to weeks, and repeated requests do not accelerate it. [Recrawl guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl).

Record a baseline and review weekly: indexed submitted URLs, exclusion reasons, impressions/clicks by landing page, both brand spellings, and specific player-task queries. Evaluate progress over the first 4–8 weeks without treating that interval as an indexing deadline. Compare like-for-like queries and devices; a single broad wiki query cannot represent the whole opportunity.

The defensible goal is to be the most useful, current and reliably discoverable reference for these player tasks. The evidence supports technical advantages over the JavaScript-only catalogues, but not a claim that this project already outranks every competitor.
